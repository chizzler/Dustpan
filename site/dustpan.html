Task manager · Linux · Mac

Private, free task management

A free, private solution for task management. Dustpan is a native task manager for Linux and Mac. Everything lives on your machine. It works on its own, or syncs through an opensource server you run wherever you like. when you want your other devices in step.

Features

Projects, repeats, filters. All of it offline.

Dustpan covers the habits people move to Todoist for: projects, sections, subtasks, repeats and saved filters. It's written natively for each desktop, so it starts fast and feels like part of the system.

Projects

Nested as deep as you like

Projects hold sub-projects, sections and tasks. Give each one a colour and an icon, and drag to reorder or nest.

every!

Repeats that wait for you

every 3 days runs on the calendar. every! 3 days counts from when you finish, so a late task never piles up.

Quick add

Write it the way you'd say it

Dates, times, repeats, priority, project, labels and durations, pulled from one line of text. Works offline.

Subtasks

Progress you can see

A pie on each parent fills as subtasks are done. Tick the last one and Dustpan offers to finish the parent.

Views

Today, Upcoming, Anytime

Plus Completed, and saved views built from a filter language, like p1 & overdue.

Calendar

Month, Week and Day

Drag tasks onto a day or a time slot. Stretch a block to set how long it takes. Every move has a key.

Reminders

Desktop notifications

Fixed times or relative to the due date. They move when the due date moves.

Notes

Markdown on every task

Lists, links and headings in a task's notes, stored as plain text you can read anywhere.

Undo

Nothing is lost by accident

Undo for every edit, and a Trash that keeps deleted tasks and projects until you restore or clear them.

Sync

Works alone. Syncs when you say.

Every device keeps a full copy of your tasks and works without a connection. When you want sync, point Dustpan at your own OpenTodo server. Edits merge field by field, so two devices can change one task without losing either change.

Linux

Omarchy desktop

GTK 4 and libadwaita. Follows your Omarchy theme as it changes.

Your server

OpenTodo

Open source, one small binary. It holds the log of changes and passes them on. Your clients do the rest.

macOS

Mac

SwiftUI. Same data, same rules, checked against the same tests.

  1. Start on your own

    Install and start typing. No account, no sign-up, no server.

  2. Connect when ready

    Add your server's address and a token in Settings. Dustpan checks it, then uploads what you've made.

  3. Stay in step

    Changes go out a moment after you make them and arrive live on your other devices.

Privacy

Your tasks stay in your house.

A task list knows a lot about you: where you're going, who you owe and what's worrying you. Dustpan keeps that between your devices and your server.

No telemetry

No analytics, no crash reporting, no accounts with us. Nothing leaves your machine unless you set up sync.

Your server, your rules

Sync runs through a server you host. Lose it and you lose nothing: every device holds the whole list.

Encrypted in transit

Dustpan refuses plain http to any machine but your own, and won't follow a redirect, so your token can't leak.

A short supply chain

No PyPI packages on Linux and no third-party Swift packages on the Mac. Less code you didn't ask for.

Platforms

Native on every desk.

Each client is written for its own system, not wrapped in a browser. They share one data model and one set of tests.
